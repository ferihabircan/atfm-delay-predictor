How late will this departure be, and how sure are we?

A gradient-boosting model that predicts departure delay, the probability of a delay over 15 minutes, and an 80% prediction interval for flights at eight major European hubs, using the drivers that matter in ATFM: demand/capacity, weather and reactionary delay.

Approach

The pipeline is built so that the numbers can be trusted: a fixed data schema, a chronological split, a simple baseline to beat, and explanations for every model.

1

Flight-level data

185k departures with airport, hour, aircraft type, demand/capacity ratio, wind, visibility, thunderstorms and the previous leg's delay. Real data in the same schema (e.g. EUROCONTROL R&D archive, OpenSky) can be plugged in with --data.

2

No look-ahead

Train on the first 80% of days and test on the last 20%. Shuffled splits leak same-day weather and congestion into training and overstate accuracy.

3

Three models, one baseline

A median regressor (point estimate), a classifier (P(delay > 15 min)) and two quantile regressors (P10, P90), all compared with the historical mean delay per airport and hour.

Results on the test period

Errors are measured on 38,550 flights the model never saw, all later in time than the training data.

What drives the predictions

Increase in error (minutes) when a feature is shuffled. Higher = more important.

Results are on a synthetic but structurally realistic dataset, so the absolute numbers will differ on real traffic. The ranking of drivers matches the structure of CODA delay-cause reporting (capacity, weather, reactionary).

Limitations and next steps

Limitations

  • Synthetic data. Built to mimic real delay structure, but not real traffic.
  • Day-level weather. Real thunderstorms change hour by hour.
  • No regulation data. The model does not know which ATFM regulations were active.

Next steps

  • Real data: train on the EUROCONTROL Aviation Data Repository for Research.
  • METAR/TAF: parse hourly weather reports into features.
  • Regulations: add the regulated sector and reason code as inputs.
